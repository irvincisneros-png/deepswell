## 2. Fun and game feel

- **The novice surf loop is brittle.** In fixed-randomness probes, holding W from the start and waiting for POP UP never produced that prompt; it ended “Caught inside” with no ride. Waiting until a Paddle prompt instead produced a successful 11.07-second, 753-point ride. Give novices a protected first wave, a stable lineup and explicit “wait / paddle / stand” phases. The relevant push/catch logic is `src/surf/surfSession.js:68–101`.
- **The game can punish before the player understands the threat.** The existing result image records a **0.3-second** ride ending because the lip hit the surfer's head. Barrel detection kills immediately once its thresholds are met; there is no crouch lead-in prompt in that path (`src/surf/surfSession.js:168–176`). Add a visible approaching lip cue and a short first-ride reaction allowance. This screenshot is one prior attempt, not a measured failure rate.
- **Success can also feel automatic.** The prompt-following probe rode to the lagoon without A/D. The low-face position is clamped to −0.3 after each update, while its positional finish condition requires less than −0.35. At 60 Hz, even the maximum downward step (13 m/s over a minimum 4.5 m face length) cannot bridge that gap (`src/surf/surfSession.js:159`, `179–185`). Fix the threshold/clamp relationship; make dropping onto the flats have a clear, recoverable consequence.
- **“W pump” hides a change of input grammar.** Paddling uses hold; pumping uses a fresh key press plus cooldown. Holding W yields one pump, not repeated pumping (`src/surf/surfSession.js:67`, `145`). Teach “tap W rhythmically” or support hold-to-pump assistance.
- **Steering needs to be explained in the play view.** A/D mean lower/higher on the wave face, rather than ordinary left/right turning. Help explains this, the HUD says only “steer” (`src/ui/panels.js:256`; `src/main.js:147`). Show a face-position indicator and a safe shoulder target.
- **Surfing lacks direct exit agency.** Kick-out occurs through a sustained climb over the lip or automatic end conditions (`src/surf/surfSession.js:175–180`). Offer a clearly taught voluntary kick-out/dive action.
- **Dive exploration needs landmarks and board guidance.** Board distance/direction appears only after pressing E away from it (`src/main.js:233–242`). A persistent surface compass marker and discoverable cleaning-station landmark would reduce aimless searching. Actual navigation difficulty remains unmeasured.

## 3. Visual gaps, ranked against the realism brief

The menu typography and panel hierarchy are coherent. The world shown in the screenshots reads as a procedural browser prototype. More bloom or more polygons alone will not bridge that gap.

| Rank | Gap | Concrete technical direction |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The hero surfer is visibly assembled from simple shapes.** The paddle/ride images expose capsule limbs and a simple sphere head. The pop-up still looks prone during its transition. | Replace the primitive assembly (`src/surf/surfer.js:64–88`) with a properly skinned glTF character, plausible anatomy, wet suit/skin material maps and authored paddle/pop-up/stance/crouch animations. Retain foot/hand IK for board contact; review animation timing against collision timing. |
| **2** | **The reef lacks convincing material and habitat detail.** The ride image shows sparse, repeated branching colonies, flat plates and strongly repeated sand relief. | Use a small, high-quality library of coral/rock/rubble assets with baked normal/AO/roughness maps, biologically plausible clusters and multiple LODs. Keep instancing. Current colonies largely use vertex colour plus uniform roughness (`src/world/reef.js:48`, `74`); sand reuses one greyscale map as colour and a strong bump map (`src/world/terrain.js:61–64`). |
| **3** | **Water looks like a tinted surface with a detached foam accent.** Fix the cutout defect before tuning it. | Add depth-based refraction and scene-colour sampling, coherent foam advection, persistent whitewater/wake trails and convincing lip thickness. Current reflections sample the procedural sky; visibility through water is primarily alpha blending (`src/ocean/water.js:105–131`). Reserve expensive passes for high quality. |
| **4** | **Lighting lacks grounding and photographic depth.** Screenshot objects do not have convincing contact shading. | Establish a reference exposure/colour grade, add selective sun shadows/contact AO and coherent underwater light attenuation. Set a benchmark scene first: one surfer, one breaking wave and one dense reef/cleaning-station close-up. Approve those at both quality tiers before expanding the world.

## 4. Science accuracy and misleading simplifications

1. **The lab bleaches an explicitly non-photosymbiotic sea fan.** All reef generators except rock receive the bleaching shader, including `seafan` and `sponge` (`src/world/reef.js:75`; `src/ocean/shaders.js:114–119`). The Annella card correctly says it has no symbiotic algae (`src/content/gbr.js:704`). Use taxon-specific susceptibility and distinguish heat injury from loss of symbionts. This is an internal content/simulation contradiction.
2. **Fishing/grazing responses contradict the species cards.** Moorish idols and threadfin butterflyfish are lumped into the lab's herbivore abundance group (`src/main.js:229–230`), although their cards describe omnivory, sponge feeding and invertebrate feeding (`src/content/gbr.js:98–111`, `287–300`). Derive functional groups from reviewed ecological metadata, with explicit simplifications.
3. **Crown-of-thorns causation is too certain.** The model directly drives COTS growth from nutrients, and the quiz explanation presents runoff → blooms → more surviving starfish as the explanation (`src/learn/reefModel.js:47–49`, `69`; `src/content/gbr.js:848–856`). Teach this as a proposed contributing mechanism with interacting drivers. The [Reef Authority's Outlook report](https://outlookreport.gbrmpa.gov.au/threats-responses-and-risks/6-factors-influencing-regions-values/65-land-based-runoff/652-vulnerability-ecosystem-land-based-runoff) explicitly says nutrient enrichment's role in outbreak dynamics remains unresolved.
4. **DHW is a constrained scenario, presented too much like a general calculator.** `dhw(temp)` assumes exactly six hot weeks and jumps from 0 at +0.9°C to 6 at +1.0°C (`src/learn/reefModel.js:8–14`). The thresholded accumulation is intentional, but the quiz describes a 12-week measure while the UI does not show an exposure timeline (`src/content/gbr.js:845`). Label the six-week assumption in the controls and show heat-stress accumulation. [NOAA defines DHW over the preceding 12 weeks, accumulating qualifying HotSpots](https://www.coralreefwatch.noaa.gov/product/5km/index_5km_dhw.php).
5. **The physiology teaches a dependable countdown that the safety text says does not exist.** Breath is a linear effort timer, refills at 22 percentage points/second, and blackout has no ascent-pressure component (`src/dive/diver.js:87–106`). Surface recovery from empty takes about 4.5 seconds. The quiz correctly discusses ascent blackout (`src/content/gbr.js:893`). Label the gauge as a game reserve, avoid implying a safe real-world timer, and distinguish hypoxia from CO₂-driven urge to breathe. [DAN describes particular risk late in ascent and shortly after surfacing](https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/hypoxia-in-breath-hold-diving/).
6. **Pain should not be taught as “press Q and continue”.** The event prompt says exactly that (`src/main.js:503`), while `Q` instantly clears any ear load (`src/dive/diver.js:103`). Teach stop descent, ascend slightly, equalise gently, and abort if it fails. [DAN advises aborting a dive when pain occurs](https://dan.org/health-medicine/health-resource/dive-medical-reference-books/ears-diving/ears-equalization/). The probe stalled near 3 m when ignoring equalisation, which is useful feedback; it needs the right explanation.
7. **One clear bibliographic error:** cleaner-wrasse mirror research is cited as “Kohda et al. 2019, PNAS” (`src/content/gbr.js:144`); the [2019 paper was published in PLOS Biology](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.3000021). Provide actual links/DOIs and assessment dates, rather than only source names. I have not independently revalidated every conservation status.

The approximate pressure relationship (2 atm at 10 m) is appropriate. A fixed neutral-buoyancy depth of 10 m is acceptable as a disclosed game assumption, but varies with diver, equipment and weighting (`src/dive/diver.js:8–14`).

## 5. Teaching, UX, accessibility and performance

- **The lab graph misrepresents its values.** Its shared axis is labelled 0–100%, but bleaching and COTS are multiplied by 0.5: full bleaching plots at 50%. COTS is a normalised index, not a defined percentage density. There is no labelled time axis (`src/ui/panels.js:195–204`). Separate cover percentages from indices, include units and year ticks, and offer a text/table export.
- **The lab cannot be seen clearly while it runs.** It occupies a modal; the reef behind is blurred by the modal backdrop (`styles.css:110`). Apply results, close to inspect, and reopening the lab reinitialises controls/chart rather than recalling the applied scenario (`src/ui/panels.js:157–158`, `206`). Save scenario/results and provide an explicit compare-and-inspect workflow. The mission promises seeing algae inside coral, but the lab only changes whole-colony shading (`src/content/gbr.js:821–823`; `src/ocean/shaders.js:114–119`). Add the promised polyp/symbiont view or rewrite the task.
- **Completion is not evidence of understanding.** Probe: three wrong answers still saved `complete:true, score:0`; completed missions no longer offer the quiz button (`src/ui/panels.js:114`, `128–134`). Allow explanation review/retry and distinguish attempted, objective met and understanding demonstrated. The surf objective also accepts any ride above one point even though pop-up immediately awards 50 (`src/content/gbr.js:721`; `src/surf/surfSession.js:121–122`).
- **Teacher evidence export is incomplete.** CSV contains journal/transect rows, not mission scores, answers or lab inputs/results (`src/core/save.js:22–33`). The teacher page names transect skills, but no playable transect workflow appears in the game controls/panels (`teacher.html:33`). Add assessable observations and a local export with task, evidence and result; do not imply those workflows already exist.
- **Photo collection is not yet a census.** The mission requires five distinct fish IDs; the brief also asks students to record diet, but that is not captured or assessed by its goal (`src/content/gbr.js:757–758`; `src/main.js:252–259`). Add a simple observation record and distinguish species richness from abundance. Five-fish storage bookkeeping and the near-surface E return condition passed isolated probes; actual discoverability and photography are unverified.
- **Default reading pressure disadvantages slower readers.** Freeze physiology in cards by default; retain challenge mode as a conscious choice (`src/core/settings.js:14`). Journal-to-card navigation also replaces the journal without a Back action (`src/ui/panels.js:92–96`). Preserve location/filter and add Back.
- **Laptop accessibility needs more than readable panels.** No alternate keyboard look, key remapping or pointer-lock failure guidance is present. Sliders use adjacent labels without `for` associations (`src/ui/panels.js:165–170`). Repair keyboard focus first, label controls, offer adjustable UI text and high-contrast/non-colour-only graph legends. The HUD's many 12px instructions need checking at 1366×768 and browser zoom (`styles.css:99`, `106`).
- **Low/high performance is unmeasured.** Low reduces render resolution to 0.75 and coral density to 0.5; high uses 1.25 and bloom (`src/core/settings.js:5–7`). Low therefore changes ecological encounter density as well as fidelity. Preserve core mission habitats at every tier. CPU wave vertices/normals are rebuilt each frame (`src/ocean/water.js:218–238`), schooling scans all agents (`src/life/life.js:377–388`), and instanced fish culling is disabled (`src/life/life.js:98`). Profile frame time/long tasks on actual integrated-GPU laptops; use spatial neighbour grids, habitat chunks, LOD and adaptive resolution. Do not claim 30/60 FPS based on these Node tests.

## 6. Top 10 prioritised fixes

1. **Restore keyboard UI operation and robust mouse capture.** Acceptance: title, settings, missions, quiz and dialogs work using Tab/Shift+Tab/Enter/Space/Escape; denied pointer lock gives a usable fallback.
2. **Make dive/card/blackout state consistent.** Pause reading by default; reset warnings and camera state; stop control during blackout and cancel stale respawn callbacks.
3. **Make the first surf ride learnable.** Protected first wave, clear paddle/pop-up timing and barrel warning; reconcile hold/tap W. Validate novice success with actual students.
4. **Fix lab runs and graph semantics.** One run at a time, immutable results, correctly scaled and labelled axes, scenario persistence and visible comparison.
5. **Correct lab ecology and safety messaging.** No zooxanthellae bleaching for Annella/sponges; reviewed functional groups; nuanced COTS explanation; clear game-only physiology assumptions.
6. **Fix wave/ocean compositing.** Coordinate-independent hole enablement, then inspect barrel interior, surface transitions, refraction and foam at low/high.
7. **Deliver a realistic surfer and animation set.** Replace capsule anatomy; verify feet/board contact and pop-up/crouch animation against failure timing.
8. **Build one convincing reef/animal benchmark scene.** Authored assets, detailed materials, habitat composition and grounded lighting; retain instancing/LODs for laptops.
9. **Repair learning evidence.** Accurate interaction participants, feedback for unsupported photo subjects, quiz review/retry and local mission/lab export.
10. **Run the full real-Chrome acceptance matrix on school hardware.** Several novice surf attempts, crouched barrel, kick-out, >10 m dive with/without equalising, low O₂/blackout/recovery, board return, five fish plus cleaning, cards/journal/quizzes/lab, teacher links, keyboard-only menus, 1366×768/zoom, and both quality tiers. Review every captured image and record frame-time percentiles and console errors.
